Graviton-induced decoherence of a quantum point-particle is expected to happen only for macroscopic systems whose masses are much larger than the Planck mass, due to the weakness of the gravitational interaction. In this seminar, I will discuss how the situation changes for composite systems, namely those described by dynamical external and internal degrees of freedom (DoFs). By choosing the external DoFs (like the center-of-mass coordinates) to be the system of interest, the internal motion acts as an environment which inevitably also interacts with the gravitational field. The mutually interacting gravitational and internal environments are integrated out by using the Feynman-Vernon approach to open quantum systems, leading to decoherence in the external DoFs of the composite particle. I will show that this interplay between environments acts as an amplifier of graviton-induced decoherence, making it possible for this mechanism to happen for long times even for microscopic systems with masses much smaller than the Planck mass.
