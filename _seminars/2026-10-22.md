The close similarities of the three laws of black hole mechanics with the laws of thermodynamics led to the identification of event horizon area with black hole entropy. However, this paradigm has some important limitations, leading to difficulties with identifying entropy in dynamical situations. After a brief review of these limitations, we will show that they can be overcome with quasi-local horizons. In particular, using dynamical horizons segments (DHSs), we extend the first law to encompass black holes that can be arbitrarily far from equilibrium. It now refers to finite changes that occur due to physical processes. This extension, together with the second law on DHSs, naturally lead one to identify black hole entropy with the area of marginally trapped surfaces in quasi-local horizons.
